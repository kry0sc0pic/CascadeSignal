# CascadeSignal paper figures

Generated by `scripts/paper/*.py`. Regenerate all with:

```bash
set -a && source .env && set +a
uv run python scripts/paper/build_all.py
```

Each figure also writes a `.pdf` (vector) + `.png` (300 dpi); Figs 2–7 also
write a `.json` with the underlying numbers.

## Conventions shared across all figures

- **Signal.** The univariate Hawkes branching ratio n(t) ∈ [0,1) on each
  protocol's per-block liquidation-count stream (`bar_blocks = 1`), scored from
  the start of history by the **persisted live fit** in `data/live_state/<p>.json`
  — i.e. the exact model the live monitor runs. (Exceptions: Fig 2 refits
  walk-forward at `bar_blocks = 5`; Fig 8's Compound/Maker panels use a
  full-sample fit.)
- **Operating point** (env override → `thresholds.json`, as `live/config.py`
  resolves it): **Aave v2** threshold 0.99890, **Aave v3** threshold 0.9998,
  both debounce **k = 10**. A debounced alarm fires once per run of k
  consecutive above-threshold bars (`live/monitor.py`). v3's threshold was
  raised from its calibrated 0.99958 to 0.9998 (ADR-006) to filter a lone
  precursor-spike artifact ~192 min before the Feb 2025 onset; at 0.9998 v3's
  FAR more than halves (0.42→0.17/week) and the major cascade is still caught,
  at the cost of 9 of 45 minor episodes (recall_all 1.00→0.80).
- **Detection rule (paper-wide).** An alarm is a **true warning** of a cascade
  if it fires in `[start − LEAD_WINDOW, end]` (early *or* during the cascade);
  a **false alarm** fires outside *every* labelled cascade's window.
  `LEAD_WINDOW = 1200` blocks (~4.2 h), comfortably covering the observed
  ≤213-min leads. This is more permissive than `eval/alerts.py`'s strict
  `[start−50, start)` forecast window (which counts an early alarm as "too
  early"); it is the right accounting for an early-warning claim, and is a
  documented modelling choice, not the native horizon.
- **Lead time (ADR-006).** Two distinct, separately-labelled metrics — never
  conflated: **advance warning** = `cascade start − alarm` (how far before the
  cascade *begins* the alarm fires; the honest early-warning number) and
  **time-to-50 %-cost** = `50 %-of-USD block − alarm` (the alarm→½-cost figure,
  which ties to protectable USD). Per cascade (advance / to-½-cost): Dec 2021
  **1 / 14 min**, Jun 2022 **10 / 30**, May 2021 **53 / 76**, Feb 2025
  **onset / 18** (v3 fires at onset — see the Operating-point note on the 0.9998
  threshold).
- **Data.** Aave v2: 2020-12 → 2026-02 (13.1 M blocks, 49 k liquidations, USD
  priced). Aave v3: 2023-01 → 2026-02 (8.0 M blocks). Compound v2 / Maker have
  liquidation counts only (no USD, no persisted fit).

## Fig 1 — full-history detection + quiet strip (`fig1_full_history`)
**Claim:** the alarm fires early on real cascades and rarely otherwise.
**Top:** each major D-A cascade cropped to its window — n(t) through threshold,
the debounced alarm (▼), cumulative liquidation $ underneath; the panels
annotate **time-to-50%-cost** (May 2021 +75 min, Jun 2022 +23, Dec 2021 +14,
Feb 2025 +18) — the alarm→½-cost metric, not the advance warning (see the
ADR-006 convention above for both numbers).
**Bottom:** the same operating point over each full history as a monthly
false-alarm count vs. the 1/week budget. **Numbers (both k=10):** v2 125 false =
0.46/week; v3 28 false = 0.17/week — both comfortably under budget, with false
alarms clustered in a few genuinely volatile months and long quiet gaps.

## Fig 2 — Hawkes vs. naive baselines (`fig2_baselines`)
**Claim:** n(t) beats "just threshold the liquidation rate." Walk-forward
out-of-fold (each bar scored by a model trained only on earlier months),
`bar_blocks = 5`, all labelled D-A episodes as positives. **Left:** AUPRC lift
over base rate — Hawkes wins both (v2 **209×** vs 140–181×; v3 **402×** vs
233–324×). **Right:** OOF precision–recall — Hawkes at/above every baseline,
clearest at high recall. **Caveat:** absolute AUPRC is low (rare-event regime,
~80–90 positive bars); this figure ranks detectors, it is not the live
operating point (Figs 1/3).

## Fig 3 — operating characteristics (`fig3_operating_characteristics`)
**Claim:** the threshold is principled and the warning is real. **Left:**
false-alarm/recall trade-off swept over threshold at the deployed k, operating
point starred: v2 catches **3/3 major cascades** (0.86 recall of all 98 D-A
episodes) at **0.46/week**; v3 **1/1** major (0.80 recall of 45) at **0.17/week**,
both under the 1/week budget. **Right:** early-warning lead per cascade, as the
two ADR-006 metrics — **advance warning** (minutes before the first liquidation)
and **time-to-50 %-cost** (minutes before 50 % of USD is liquidated), e.g.
Feb 2025 **onset / 18 min** (v3 fires at onset); Dec 2021 a short **1 / 14 min**.

## Fig 4 — out-of-sample false-alarm rate by year (`fig4_oos_far`)
**Claim:** not overfit. Threshold calibrated on 2021–22 (v2) / 2025 (v3); other
years are unseen quiet stretches. **Numbers:** pooled OOS FAR **v2 0.28/week**,
**v3 0.21/week**; OOS years are not systematically worse than in-sample.
**Caveat (honest):** v3's partial-year 2026 bar (~1.4/week over ~8 weeks) sits
above budget — it is the acute Feb 2026 market-wide deleveraging, a genuinely
active window, and some of those fires land on real stress that the v3 label
set (which ends 2026-02-28) may not fully cover, so this rate is if anything an
overcount of *false* alarms.

## Fig 5 — protectable USD (`fig5_protectable_usd`)
**Claim:** the alarm gets ahead of most of the money. **Left:** per cascade,
USD already liquidated at the alarm vs. still ahead of it — **72–82 % ahead**
(v3 Feb 2025 79 %). **Right:** total `severity_usd` warned on *in time* (a
debounced fire strictly before start) over all labelled cascades, 95 % episode
bootstrap CI: **v2 $3.08B / $4.11B = 75 %** [$2.49B, $3.68B]; **v3
$936M / $5.77B = 16 %** [$401M, $1.52B]. v3's in-time share is low because its
alarm now fires *at* onset (0.9998 threshold), not before it — the per-cascade
"still ahead" number (79 %) shows it is still early *within* the cascade.
**Caveat:** an upper bound — assumes a perfect instant intervention on every
timely alarm; not USD actually saved.

## Fig 6 — debounce (k) ablation (`fig6_debounce_ablation`)
**Claim:** the persistence filter is a real knee, not a free lunch. Sweeping k
at the deployed threshold, both protocols at **k = 10** (dashed line): **v2**
FAR falls 0.89→0.46/week (k=1→10) for only ~6 min of lead, collapsing to
negative lead by k≈30 — k=10 is a sensible knee. **v3** (at the raised 0.9998
threshold) FAR falls 0.48→0.17/week (k=1→10); the sweep also shows the debounce
itself filtering the lone precursor spike — advance "lead" reads ~193 min at
k=1–2 (the artifact fires) then drops to ~0 (onset) from k≈3 on, which is why
k=10 reports onset detection. The major cascade is still caught through k=20.

## Fig 7 — Hawkes goodness-of-fit (`fig7_hawkes_gof`)
**Claim:** the model is well-specified. Intensity calibration: mean observed
liquidations vs. mean predicted intensity λ per log-λ bin track y=x across ~4
orders of magnitude, tightest in the high-intensity regime the alarm uses (mild
under-prediction at the very lowest λ). Stationarity: exponential-kernel
branching ratio **0.83 (v2) / 0.79 (v3) < 1**. PIT-residual KS distance is tiny
(D ≈ 0.0009 / 0.0007); the significant p-values are the N = 8–13 M artifact
(effect size, not misfit).

## Fig 8 — cross-protocol transfer (`fig8_cross_protocol`)
**Claim:** the signal is not Aave-specific. The same univariate n(t) built on
each protocol's own liquidation stream saturates (peak n(t) ≈ 0.999–1.000) into
its largest cascade across four lending designs — Aave v2/v3 (persisted fit),
Compound v2 (cToken model) and Maker (CDP/urn model), the latter two a
full-sample fit with a 99.9th-pct reference level, illustrative of the signal
rather than a tuned detector.

## Deliberately out of scope

**Graph-fragility covariate ablation** (Hawkes-only vs. Hawkes + contagion-graph
fragility, via `hawkes.cov_mode="excitation"` + `graph/fragility.py`). This is
the paper's most novel potential contribution but is currently Aave-v2-only and
extending it to the other protocols is the multi-day, per-protocol data work in
`CLAUDE.md`. Include it only if the paper's thesis is *"graph structure improves
early warning"* rather than *"the branching ratio is a good early-warning
signal"* (which Figs 1–8 already support).
